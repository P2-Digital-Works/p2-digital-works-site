# Test the site with prospective clients

The client-usability revision is informed by research. No target-user study or conversion improvement has been measured yet. Browser checks and code tests establish functionality, not whether unfamiliar visitors understand or trust the site.

## First round

Recruit six likely prospective clients: roughly half comfortable with digital tools, half less confident. Include phone and computer use and participants who use browser-based email. This is a practical qualitative sample to find problems, not a statistically representative experiment. GOV.UK suggests [4–8 participants per research round](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/plan-user-research-for-your-service).

Run 20–30 minute individual sessions. Explain: “We are testing the website, not you. Please say what you are thinking. I may wait before helping so we can see where the site needs to be clearer.” Use invented project details; do not collect passwords or sensitive business information. Ask permission before recording. Do not ask participants to send real inquiries to the production inbox; use a controlled test inbox for the final sending task.

## Tasks without coaching

1. Show the homepage briefly. Hide it and ask: “What does this business do? Who would it help?” Record their words, not a yes/no rating.
2. “Imagine you want customers to browse your shop's products online. Find out whether this business could help and what you would do next.”
3. “You like one of the examples. Find out what was actually built and whether it was for a paying customer.”
4. “You have an idea but do not know what kind of website or tool you need. Show how you would start a conversation.”
5. After the draft appears: “What has happened so far? Has the business received anything? What would you do next?”
6. “You use email in your browser. Show how you would get this message ready to send.” In the controlled test, verify receipt and the reply path.
7. “You notice a mistake in your description. Correct it and prepare the message again.” Verify they do not reuse an old draft.

Use one open follow-up at the end: “What, if anything, made you hesitate or feel unsure?” Avoid asking only whether they liked the design.

Also record first navigation choice, task time, misunderstood terms and whether the participant thought preparing/copying meant sending. Keep identifiable contact details out of this log.

Fix blockers and any misleading delivery interpretation first, then recurring comprehension problems. Do not average away a serious problem that affected one participant. Run another small round after meaningful changes, preferably with fresh participants. With six participants, report counts and observations rather than claiming statistical significance.

If traffic later becomes sufficient, compare changes with a planned experiment and an adequate sample. Useful business outcomes include successfully received inquiries, inquiry completion and qualified conversations. Avoid optimizing raw button clicks at the expense of client understanding. No analytics or experiment platform was added by this revision.
